\paper{Minimum Wage Effects on Youth Education in a Dual Labor Market: Evidence from Thailand.}{}{%
I study how Thailand's 300 Baht minimum wage policy affected the educational choices of youth aged 15--17. Using provincial variation in the size of the increase in a generalized difference-in-differences design, I find no overall effect on enrollment but a shift in composition: full-time enrollment rises and the share of youth working and not enrolled falls. The response is concentrated among female students and is consistent with disemployment effects and with differences in adult labor market outcomes by education.}

\paper{Competitive Federalism and Development: Evidence from India's Aspirational Districts Programme.}{ With Shantanu Khanna.}{%
We study India's Aspirational Districts Programme, launched in 2018 to accelerate development in the country's least developed districts. Using synthetic difference-in-differences on nationally representative data from 2009--2023, we find the program raised labor force participation, driven by rural areas and women, and increased household consumption while reducing poverty. Treated districts also urbanized more slowly and shifted from regular wage work toward self-employment, mainly in the primary sector.}

\paper{Artificial Intelligence and Endogenous Productivity: Evidence from Korean Firms.}{ With [First name] Wang.}{%
We study how AI adoption affects firm productivity in South Korea using a 2017--2023 panel covering all major sectors, combining direct survey measures of adoption with a structural model of endogenous productivity to address selection. Adoption raises revenue productivity by about 5\% on average, following a delayed J-curve, with the largest gains in ICT and short-run losses in manufacturing. We also examine how adoption relates to market power.}

\paper{Open to All? Diversity Statements in Job Advertisements and Hiring Discrimination.}{ With [First name] Granberg, Shantanu Khanna, and [First name] Sinclair.}{%
Using nearly 11,500 job advertisements in Sweden, we characterize which jobs and firms include diversity statements and test whether these statements are associated with less hiring discrimination. A correspondence study finds lower callbacks for ethnic minorities and higher callbacks for women. Discrimination appears lower for ads with diversity statements, but the gap disappears once job and employer characteristics are balanced.}
